# Homework after lesson 21 - final project proposal

No coding this weekend. Instead you write down your final project so that it
can be approved on Monday (session 22) and started on Friday (session 23).

## The task

1. Copy `final-project-template.md` to
   `students/<your-username>/final-project.md` in the cohort repository.
2. Fill in every section. Section 9 (open questions) is the only place
   where "TBD" is allowed.
3. Commit and push before **Monday 2026-11-16, 12:00**, so there is time to
   read all four before the session. Commit message:
   `Final project proposal: <project name>`.

## What gets checked on Monday

- Section 4 and 5: can it be built from hardware that exists by Friday, and
  does the pin budget hold up (strapping pins, 5 V tolerance, nothing used
  twice)?
- Section 8: is the minimum really reachable in two evenings? A minimum that
  needs WiFi + display + three sensors + MQTT is not a minimum.
- Section 6: is the behaviour concrete enough that we both know what "done"
  means on demo day?

Proposals that pass get "Status: approved" and you start wiring on Friday.
Proposals that do not pass get a 10-minute conversation on Monday and a
corrected version by Wednesday.

## Hints

- Build on something the course already touched: AHT10, Nokia 5110, RGB LED,
  buttons, potentiometer, WS2812, WiFi, MQTT, ESP-NOW, deep sleep, CAN. New
  hardware is allowed but is your risk, not the course's.
- Think of your minimum as "session 15 homework plus one idea of mine". That
  is the right size.
- A project that works and is small beats a project that is ambitious and
  half-wired on demo day. The grading is on the demo and on the write-up,
  not on the size of the parts list.
- Individual or paired: both are fine. Paired projects need two sections 10
  (one per person) so it is clear who does what.
